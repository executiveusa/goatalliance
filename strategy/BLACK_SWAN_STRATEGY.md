# Black Swan Strategy — PNW AI Directory

## Decision

Add pressure washers as the fifth launch niche. The five verticals are now plumbers, HVAC contractors, roofers, painters, and pressure washers.

## Core Black Swans

### 1. The hidden buyer is not the homeowner

Homeowners create urgent demand, but property managers, HOAs, real estate agents, insurance/restoration teams, utilities, and community organizations create repeatable demand.

Action: Build profile filters for commercial availability, property-manager response, insurance/restoration fit, and community-program fit.

### 2. Missing data is the product

Most contractors do not have machine-readable proof. The missing fields become the sales hook: license, service area, response rules, warranties, photos, runoff practices, rebate notes, and city-specific proof.

Action: Sell the AI Visibility Audit as the first paid product.

### 3. Pressure washing gives the strongest social-purpose bridge

Pressure washing can connect beautification, graffiti cleanup, stormwater/runoff education, storefront support, property management, and community cleanup days.

Action: Launch EcoWash Verified as a responsible-pressure-washing profile lane.

### 4. Grants are upside, not the business model

Federal environmental justice grants have become politically and legally volatile. Use grants for partner projects, not payroll dependency.

Action: Use contractor subscriptions, profile audits, sponsors, and data products as the base. Treat grants as campaign accelerators.

### 5. AI-readable profiles can become infrastructure

A normal directory sells placement. This directory can become a local service data layer: JSON-LD, markdown, llms.txt, profile feeds, verification metadata, and community-capacity maps.

Action: Build exportable profile JSON and sponsor city/category pages.

## Ethical operating rule

Do not use persuasion to trap local businesses. Use it to uncover hidden constraints, make the value clear, and let bad-fit prospects walk away clean.
