## The practical shift

Local visibility is no longer only about showing up in a list of blue links. Search engines, maps, AI assistants, and property-management workflows all need the same thing: clear proof about who serves which area, what they actually do, and what is safe to recommend.

For contractors, this creates a simple opportunity. A business that publishes clean service data, proof assets, service-area rules, and useful maintenance education is easier to understand than a competitor with a vague homepage and a few review snippets.

## What to publish

Start with the information a cautious buyer would need before calling:

- service category and exclusions
- cities and neighborhoods served
- emergency rules and response windows
- license, insurance, bond, or verification status
- proof assets tied to real services
- seasonal constraints
- warranties or limitations
- plain-language FAQs

This should be readable on the page and readable in structured data. The human page builds trust. The machine-readable layer reduces ambiguity.

## The social-purpose angle

A directory should not only extract attention from a local market. It can improve the market. Every profile audit can teach safer maintenance, cleaner practices, and better documentation. Premium profiles can subsidize smaller contractors and community projects.

The useful question is not: how do we rank fast? The better question is: what information would make a real buyer, a community partner, and an AI assistant more confident without making fake claims?

## Operating action

Hermes should turn this into a daily loop: find weak profiles, score missing proof, draft useful content, create structured data, flag risky claims, and queue human verification before anything sensitive is published.
